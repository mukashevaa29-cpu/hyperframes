How does it feel? When the asteroid that killed the dinosaurs hits the Earth.
66 million years ago. A rock ten kilometers wide.
It's moving at twenty kilometers per second.
It crosses the entire atmosphere in a few seconds.
You're a thousand kilometers away.
First, the flash. Brighter than the sun.
The impact releases more energy than billions of atomic bombs.
The ground shakes harder than in any earthquake in human history.
Within the hour, molten rock starts raining from the sky.
Every fragment glows as it falls. The air heats up like an oven.
Forests burst into flames across the continent.
On the coast, a wave over a hundred meters tall.
Then the soot rises, and blocks out the sun.
Day turns to night. For months. Maybe years.
Three quarters of all species on Earth disappear.
And then... comes darkness.
