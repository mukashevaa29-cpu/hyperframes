# Next episodes — executions with an interesting mechanism

Ranked by how good the "how it works" story is. Each entry gives the opening line, the mechanism to visualise, why it hooks, and how solid the history is.
A ⚠️ entry is a legend or disputed: say so on screen ("according to Plutarch…"). It protects credibility, and the debunk is a hook of its own.

1. **The Guillotine** (France, 1792–1977)
   - **Opening:** "How does it feel? When the blade of the guillotine drops."
   - **Mechanism:** a weighted blade of about 40 kg (with its "mouton") falls about 2.3 m down greased grooves. It is angled at 45° so it slices rather than chops. The lunette locks the neck, and the cut takes a fraction of a second.
   - **Hook:** the 1905 Languille report. A doctor claimed the head reacted to its name for about 25–30 s. Ends on the question "are you still conscious?"
   - **History:** documented. The last use was in 1977.

2. **The Iron Maiden ⚠️ (the twist episode)**
   - **Opening:** "How does it feel? When they close the iron maiden."
   - **Mechanism:** walk through the classic version, a spiked cabinet with doors closing on the body. Then reveal there is no record of it ever being used: it was almost certainly a 19th-century fabrication.
   - **Hook:** a myth-busting payoff. It drives comments.
   - **History:** legend, and the debunk is the episode.

3. **The Garrote** (Spain, until 1974)
   - **Opening:** "How does it feel? When they tighten the garrote."
   - **Mechanism:** an iron collar on a post, tightened by a screw or lever behind the neck. Some versions had a spike pointing into the spine.
   - **Hook:** used in Europe within living memory. The last use was 1974 (Salvador Puig Antich).
   - **History:** documented.

4. **The Long Drop — the maths of hanging** (Britain, 1870s)
   - **Opening:** "How does it feel? When they calculate the rope for your weight."
   - **Mechanism:** William Marwood's "long drop". The rope length is set from the body weight, so the fall snaps the neck at C2 (the "hangman's fracture") instead of slowly strangling. Too long a drop and the head comes off; too short and you strangle.
   - **Hook:** hanging turned into engineering. Visualise it as a formula, a weight and a counter.
   - **History:** documented, with British "table of drops".

5. **The Breaking Wheel** (Europe, Middle Ages–1841)
   - **Opening:** "How does it feel? When they tie you to the breaking wheel."
   - **Mechanism:** the limbs were broken with a heavy wheel, then woven between its spokes and raised on a pole.
   - **Hook:** the device was both the weapon and the display.
   - **History:** documented. Last uses were in Prussia in the 1840s.

6. **The Gas Chamber** (USA, from 1924)
   - **Opening:** "How does it feel? When the pellets drop in the gas chamber."
   - **Mechanism:** a sealed steel room. Sodium cyanide pellets are lowered into sulfuric acid under the chair, releasing hydrogen cyanide, which blocks cells from using oxygen.
   - **Hook:** a chemistry diagram plus a ticking clock. The first use was Gee Jon, Nevada, 1924.
   - **History:** documented.

7. **Keelhauling** (Dutch and other navies, 16th–18th c.)
   - **Opening:** "How does it feel? When they drag you under the ship."
   - **Mechanism:** a rope runs under the hull, and the sailor is pulled from one side to the other across the keel, over barnacles razor-sharp enough to cut flesh, while holding his breath.
   - **Hook:** a strong side-view diagram, with a breath counter and the barnacles.
   - **History:** documented but rare.

8. **Poena Cullei — the Sack** (Ancient Rome)
   - **Opening:** "How does it feel? When they sew you into a sack with a dog, a rooster, a snake and a monkey."
   - **Mechanism:** Roman law punished killing a parent this way. The convict was sewn into a leather sack with animals and thrown into water.
   - **Hook:** the strangest punishment in Roman law.
   - **History:** in the legal texts. The exact animals vary between sources.

9. **Scaphism ⚠️ — "the Boats"** (Persia)
   - **Opening:** "How does it feel? When they trap you between two boats."
   - **Mechanism:** the victim was trapped between two boats with only head and limbs exposed, force-fed milk and honey, and left to the insects.
   - **Hook:** Plutarch claims one man survived 17 days.
   - **History:** a single ancient source, likely exaggerated. Say so on screen.

10. **Pressing — "More weight"** (Salem, 1692)
    - **Opening:** "How does it feel? When they pile stones on your chest."
    - **Mechanism:** _peine forte et dure_. Heavy weights are added to a board on the chest until the prisoner enters a plea or dies.
    - **Hook:** Giles Corey refused to plead and reportedly only said "More weight."
    - **History:** documented.

11. **Death by Elephant** (South and Southeast Asia, centuries–19th c.)
    - **Opening:** "How does it feel? When the king's elephant is trained to kill you."
    - **Mechanism:** elephants trained to crush slowly or quickly on command.
    - **Hook:** the animal was the executioner.
    - **History:** documented by travellers over centuries.

12. **Blown From a Cannon** (Mughal India, then British India 1857)
    - **Opening:** "How does it feel? When they tie you to the mouth of a cannon."
    - **Mechanism:** the prisoner was tied over the muzzle of a cannon, which was then fired.
    - **Hook:** a colonial-history shock.
    - **History:** documented. Handle with care: it was used against Indian rebels, so keep it factual.

13. **The Halifax Gibbet** (England, until 1650)
    - **Opening:** "How does it feel? When the axe falls on its own."
    - **Mechanism:** a falling-axe machine used for 300 years before the guillotine.
    - **Hook:** "The guillotine was invented in England."
    - **History:** documented. A good follow-up to the guillotine episode.

14. **Crucifixion** (Rome)
    - **Opening:** "How does it feel? When they nail you to a cross."
    - **Mechanism:** death mostly from exhaustion and suffocation. To breathe out, the victim had to push up on the nailed feet.
    - **Hook:** a body-mechanics diagram.
    - **History:** documented. Sensitive for a religious audience, so keep it historical.

15. **Hanged, Drawn and Quartered** (England, for treason)
    - **Opening:** "How does it feel? When the sentence is for treason."
    - **Mechanism:** three punishments in sequence. Example: Guy Fawkes, who avoided most of it by breaking his own neck on the scaffold in 1606.
    - **History:** documented.
