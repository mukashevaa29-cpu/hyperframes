# 04_T3Pu9d7SJ6s: "13 rules for surviving in a treehouse in the zombie apocalypse"

17:54 long, 127 shots, 7.1 cuts/min, median shot 8.0 s (p10 3.0 s, p90 14.2 s). **Pop-ins 41.6/min, the highest of the four videos**; caption changes 50.7/min. No transcript was available.
This is the **siege/diary variant**: one base (a treehouse on an oak) over ~52 days. The rules come from a dead forester's notebook, and the story is told as a day count.
Sources: all 8 t-sheets, all 11 cuts sheets, 13 full-size frames in `review/` (f_2, f_30, f_82, f_92, f_125, f_175, f_242, f_330, f_475, f_510, f_615, f_872, f_1016), shots.json and frames.npz.

## 9. Pacing

- **Rate of change:** something new every ~1.4 s (pop-ins plus captions). Captions change every 1.18 s.
- **Rule spacing:** rules every ~70 s on average, but front-loaded around set pieces (Ch2 holds 3 rules in 3 min).
- **Clock-driven narration:** each beat is anchored to a day number or clock time, so on-screen counters change almost every scene, which gives constant progress.
- **Holds:** the cabin interior holds 12–20 s while tags accumulate (up to 37), and action climaxes cut every 1.2–3 s (shots 98–99, 115).

## 10. Top 10 reusable techniques

1. **Flash-forward cold open:** start on the iconic end-state image (the lit treehouse plus the horde below) with a slow 0.87× pull-out and tilt, then rewind "3 days earlier".
2. **The definition card for the threat:** a red title word, a red-X trait list, a yellow key trait, and a bite-to-turn timer on a black stage.
3. **Time-of-day as the colour script:** blue-green day, purple dusk gradient with a sun disk behind pine silhouettes, navy night with a single warm window glow, orange autumn.
4. **Day counters and digital clocks everywhere** (yellow digits on black boxes, a tear-off calendar) to drive a siege story.
5. **A diegetic notebook** (graph paper, blue handwriting, red warnings) as the rule source, ending with the hero writing **rule 14 in their own hand** (the cliffhanger rule).
6. **Named, counted antagonists by costume** (road vest, bunny pyjamas, counsellor whistle). Counting them each morning becomes a rule and a tension device.
7. **A workhorse interior** (the cabin wall with labelled props) reused for ~25% of runtime with tag lists that accumulate.
8. **The horde as a texture:** dense rows of tiny repeated figures plus a slow counter-pan, reading as a river.
9. **A recap screen listing every rule** before the end card, plus a comment question "which rule would you break first?".
10. **Light-as-threat:** the lit window as a beacon, the phone glow under a sleeping bag, a "no light at night" rule, all drawn as warm or white radial glows on dark navy.
