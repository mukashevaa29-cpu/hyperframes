## 9. Pacing

- **Event density:** about 1 new element every 1.8 s (33 pop-ins/min).

- **Two-level clock:** **days** (calendar widget, "13-Й ДЕНЬ" tags) inside **chapters**. The narration jumps whole days in one cut ("Через неделю…").

- **Breathers:** two longer bright stretches (the 14th-day "the stairwell learned to live" sequence 11:06, the 30th-day exit 16:00) relieve the night tension.

## 10. Top 10 reusable techniques

1. **Building cross-section as a story map.** A dollhouse elevation with numbered floors, a stair zigzag, the lift shaft and lit units. Return to it to locate sounds (circles plus side labels) and moving threats (the lift block).
2. **Chapter card with a chapter timeline.** "ГЛАВА N" in yellow letter-spaced caps, a white title with a red underline, and a 6-node timeline (active yellow). The narrator reads it. 2.3–3 s.
3. **Under-built shot openings.** Cut to the bare set, then pop the props in on the nouns (bottles, plates, calendar) so every cut "assembles".
4. **Day/time in the HUD pill during the cold open** ("ПОНЕДЕЛЬНИК 06:10") before chapters exist, then calendar-page widgets for days.
5. **Numbered procedure tags** that stack line by line, each in its own colour, reading out a protocol.
6. **The paper artefact as the rulebook:** the typewritten sheet, the lined list with ticking checkmarks, and a final list with one extra, newer line (the "49th flat").
7. **Neighbour ID cards:** a dark rounded card per character with a header "КВ. 12" and a one-line fate label, added one per name.
8. **Peephole fisheye framing** for "what's on the landing".
9. **Quiet constant pull-out:** almost every shot relaxes 1–4% outward, while push-ins are saved for 3 impacts.
10. **The extra creepy CTA:** after "subscribe", give the viewer a real-world action that extends the scare ("check how many flats your stairwell has").

**Craft mistakes not to copy:**

- The bedroom's red rug sits exactly behind the caption, so in every bedroom shot the caption looks boxed in a red-outlined plate. That is inconsistent with all other shots.
- Rule-card numerals are sometimes caught as a pure smear on the t-sheets (8:30, 11:24, 13:39): the build is so fast that a paused frame shows no number.
- Tag text at 1.8% of frame height in pastel boxes on pastel walls (blue tag on blue bathroom tiles at 3:33) loses contrast.
- Crowd scenes of 10+ identical-scale people standing in a row read as a police line-up. There is no depth staging.
- Rules 17–20 are crammed into 53 s, so the last rules get almost no illustration (rule 20's card is followed by just one shot of the door before the twist starts).
